# Chat card — Koopman Distillation Model (KDM)

**Paper:** *One-Step Offline Distillation of Diffusion-based Models via Koopman Modeling.* arXiv:[2505.13358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13358). Code: `github.com/azencot-group/KDM`.

**Use this for Profile A.** It is the efficiency result. Say “Koopman” only if he asks how the one-step map is parameterized.

**Authors on the preprint:** Nimrod Berman, Moshe Eliasof (Cambridge), Hedi Zisling, Ilan Naiman, Omri Azencot. Do not claim you wrote every line. Say what you decided: offline rather than online, a linear map in a learned embedding, and the comparison to GET.

---

## One sentence

A student generates the teacher’s clean sample from its noise in one forward pass, by encoding the noise, applying one learned linear map, and decoding. The teacher is not queried during student training.

## What is expensive

Deterministic reverse sampling. The teacher defines a velocity field \(f_\theta(x_t, t)\). A sample is an ODE from Gaussian noise \(x_T\) back to \(x_0\). EDM uses 35 function evaluations on CIFAR-10 and 79 on FFHQ and AFHQv2. KDM uses 1.

## What the student outputs

\[
x_0 \approx D_\psi(C_\eta E_\varphi(x_T))
\]

\(E_\varphi\) embeds the noisy endpoint. \(C_\eta\) is one linear map, not a step-by-step solver. \(D_\psi\) decodes to the image. A second encoder \(E_\phi\) embeds the clean image and is used only in training.

Offline means the training set is precomputed pairs \((x_T, x_0)\). After that the teacher weights are unused. That is the point: no trajectory, no extra teacher memory, and the student architecture need not copy the teacher. The cost is that the student never sees the path between the endpoints.

## The three training terms

- Reconstruction: \(D_\psi(E_\phi(x_0))\) matches \(x_0\). The clean encoder must keep the image, not the dynamics.
- Latent consistency: \(C_\eta E_\varphi(x_T)\) matches \(E_\phi(x_0)\).
- Prediction: the full one-step pipeline matches \(x_0\).

Plus a four-layer convolutional adversarial loss. The method section sets \(\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}=0.01\). The appendix weight sweep calls \(0.25\) the main-paper default and reports \(1.0\) as best (FID 4.57). Do not quote a single \(\lambda\) unless you have checked which run produced Table 1.

Conditional generation adds a control term \(C_\mu(c)\) inside the same linear step. It is not a separate diffusion model.

## Theory, said carefully

Theorem 5.1: if the flow is analytic, then for any \(\epsilon\) there is a finite linear operator on polynomial observables whose expected error is at most \(\epsilon\). The dimension grows at most polynomially in \(1/\epsilon\). This is an approximation result. It does not say the network’s latent size is the exact Koopman dimension of diffusion.

Theorem 5.2: under an invariant finite observable subspace and a locally Lipschitz flow, distance between clean samples is bounded by a Lipschitz constant times \(\|C_T\|\) times the distance of the embeddings of the two noises. Measured average spectral norm of \(C_T\): \(0.88 \pm 0.18\). The useful claim is local: nearby noises stay nearby in image space after the teacher is trained. The checkerboard figure is the picture. Untrained noise has no such clusters.

## Numbers worth memorizing

FID and IS on 50k samples. Student and the offline baselines are 1 NFE.

**Unconditional CIFAR-10**

| Model | NFE | FID | IS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EDM teacher | 35 | 2.04 | 9.84 |
| CTM (online, LPIPS) | 1 | 1.98 | — |
| GET (offline) | 1 | 6.91 | 9.16 |
| IMM, adapted offline | 1 | 4.81 | 9.16 |
| 2-rf++ without pretrain | 1 | 6.32 | 9.01 |
| **KDM (EDM)** | **1** | **4.65** | **9.21** |
| KDM (flow matching) | 1 | 5.91 | 8.86 |
| KDM-F (EDM) | 1 | 4.68 | 9.08 |

About a 25% FID drop versus GET (6.91 to 4.65). The teacher is still better. The best online one-step number in the table (CTM, 1.98) is better than KDM. Say that before he does.

**Class-conditional CIFAR-10.** GET 6.25 FID. KDM 3.56. KDM-F 3.24. ECM, an online method, is 3.81. Here the factorized student beats that online baseline. Do not mix this sentence with the unconditional table.

**Higher resolution, unconditional.** FFHQ 64: EDM 2.47 FID at 79 NFE, KDM 6.54 at 1. AFHQv2 64: EDM 2.02, KDM 4.85. FID is about 2.5× the teacher’s. GET was not run: the paper says one GET model is 5–7 weeks on an A6000. The paper does not report ImageNet. On a call, scaling stops at FFHQ and AFHQv2. Do not describe an ImageNet run. The numbers are not at hand, and a listener will ask for the FID.

**Same-noise agreement with the teacher**, not FID. On 50k samples, KDM versus EDM (79 NFE) is LPIPS 0.13 and MSE 0.009, against 0.48 and 0.57 for randomly paired images.

**Cost versus GET**, Table 4, RTX 4090, batch 128, about 62M parameters. Train step 3.60 s versus 0.91 s. Sampling 937 s versus 114 s. That is the 4× train-step and 8× sampling claim. Adding an eigen-loss to the unfactorized map slows training to 4.82 s. The factorized map with the eigen-loss stays near 0.97 s.

**Scaling.** At 84M parameters, FID 4.89 versus GET 7.19. From 250k to 1M training pairs, about a 33% FID reduction. More data still helps.

**Ablation.** The three Koopman terms together reach FID 7.83. The adversarial term is what gets the main-table FID. GET plus an adversarial loss improves GET from 6.91 to 6.13 and still loses to KDM.

Training in the appendix: 800k steps, Adam, learning rate \(3\times 10^{-4}\), no schedule, one A6000 or one 4090.

## Where he will push

1. **You did not match the teacher.** CIFAR FID 4.65 versus 2.04. One step is the product. Quality is not free.
2. **Online distillation still wins on unconditional CIFAR.** CTM at 1.98. Your win is inside offline distillation, plus the cases where the factorized conditional student beats ECM.
3. **The linear map sees only the endpoints.** It does not learn the vector field. If the path matters, this student cannot recover it from pairs alone.
4. **The finite-dimensional theorem is an approximation for analytic maps.** A learned encoder is not the polynomial basis in the proof.
5. **Resolution and scale.** Say the reported results stop at FFHQ and AFHQv2, 64×64, where the FID gap to the teacher remains about 2.5×. The strong offline baseline was too expensive to rerun there. Do not narrate an ImageNet run.
6. **The adversarial weight is easy to misquote.** Check \(\lambda\) before citing it.

## What this is not

It is not a KV-cache policy, and it is not a time-series model. The link to Ozan’s efficiency posting is: diffusion sampling cost, one evaluation instead of 35 or 79, and a student that trains without the teacher in memory. If he asks whether the same linear step would evict cache entries, the answer is no. Different object. The shared question is which computation is worth keeping.
