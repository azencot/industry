## ⭐ Five Priority Questions — Quick Reference

1. **Research Agenda:** What are the main research questions your team is currently pursuing around understanding foundation models in scientific domains?

2. **Team Needs:** Given the team’s current expertise, what kind of research background or perspective would you most like to add?

3. **Representation Formation:** I saw Masha’s post about the gap between what models represent internally and what they express. Is understanding how early training shapes this gap one of your team’s main research directions?

4. **Nick’s Research:** How does your recent work on probabilistic consistency and Bayesian belief updating connect to the team’s broader research agenda?

5. **Research Independence:** How much freedom do researchers have to define their own research questions, and what would success look like after the first year?

## Interview Strategy

* Ask questions naturally rather than reading through the list.
* Prioritize questions 1 and 2 early.
* Use question 3 if the representation gap comes up. It is a public internship post, not a claim about his req.
* Use question 4 to engage his probabilistic-consistency work.
* Question 5 covers independence and the first year. Save it for later in the conversation.
* If the conversation becomes technical, the representation–output gap is the bridge to the multimodal time-series work. That experiment is proposed, not completed.

## Positioning Reminder

Present the through-line as how models learn representations of complex data, and how architecture and objective change those representations. Recent work: connecting continuous scientific data to pretrained vision-language models.

The interest to state: what the model represents internally, how that information influences the prediction, and how training can improve reliability. Not applying an existing model to forecasting.

Do not claim established expertise in mechanistic interpretability or Bayesian inference. Emphasize independent research, experimental rigor, hands-on training, and turning an ambiguous scientific question into publishable research.
